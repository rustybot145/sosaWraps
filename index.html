02 — Process

How it runs

Four steps from the first message to driving it out in a different color.

  1. 01

    Send the car and what you want changed

    Year, make, model, and a couple of photos in daylight. Panel count and paint condition are what move the price, and photos answer both before you drive anywhere.

  2. 02

    Pick the finish, lock the date

    The finish changes the material and the material changes the number. You'll know the price and how long the car is here before you commit to either.

  3. 03

    Clean, strip, lay, tuck

    Anything in the way comes off rather than getting cut around. Vinyl goes on with heat and a squeegee, tucked behind the edges and post-heated so it stays where it's put.

  4. 04

    Pick it up in the new color

    We walk the car together in daylight before the keys go back, and you leave knowing how to wash it so it still looks like this in three years.

04 — Questions

Before you book

What people ask before they hand the keys over.

Still not sure? Ask in the DMs — no quote needed.

Will it wreck my paint?

Not on healthy factory paint. Cast vinyl sits on the clear coat, not in it, and it comes off with heat leaving the paint the way it went under — usually better protected than the panels next to it. Where it gets complicated is a resprayed panel or one with clear coat already lifting, because the film can take the weak paint with it. If yours has been painted before, say so and send a photo.

How long does a wrap last?

Three to five years is the honest range for quality cast vinyl on a daily driver, and the car's life decides where in that range you land. Garaged and hand-washed sits at the top of it; parked in the sun year-round and run through brush washes sits at the bottom. Horizontal panels — roof, hood, trunk — always go first, because that's where the sun lands hardest.

Full wrap or just a hood?

Depends what you're after. A hood, roof or chrome delete changes how the car reads from across a parking lot for a fraction of a full wrap, and it's the way most people start. A full color change is the one that makes people ask what you did to it. Both use the same material and the same install — the difference is panel count.

What tint is legal in Virginia?

Virginia allows 50% light transmission on the front side windows, and 35% on the rear sides and back glass, with a strip down to the AS-1 line on the windshield. Darker than that is a ticket, not a judgement call, so we'll settle the shade with you before anything gets cut.

Can you wrap over damage?

Vinyl follows the panel — it doesn't fill it. A dent stays a dent under gloss and reads worse, not better, because a fresh reflective surface shows every wave. Rock chips and light scratches disappear fine. Rust and flaking clear coat need sorting before the film goes anywhere near them.

How do I wash it?

Hand wash with a pH-neutral soap and two buckets, dry with a microfiber. Stay out of automatic brush washes entirely, and keep a pressure washer off the edges and seams — that's where lifting starts. Bugs and bird droppings come off sooner rather than later, especially on matte and satin.

What does it cost?

Panel count, vehicle size and finish, in that order. A chrome delete and a full color change on a Suburban aren't in the same conversation, and metallics, pearls and textures cost more per roll than a straight gloss. You get one number covering material and labor before the car is booked in — nothing gets added at pickup.

Can it come back off?

That's the point of it. Heat and patience take the film off and the factory color is still underneath, which is why a wrap makes sense on a lease or a car you plan to sell. Adhesive left behind comes off with a proper remover, not a blade.
